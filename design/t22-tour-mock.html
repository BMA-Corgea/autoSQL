Narrator — the GIMS gnome

Ruled, not proposed. autoSQL is a GIMS component, not a standalone product, so the tour uses GIMS's own gnome rather than inventing an identity. Asset: static/images/gnome-tour.png from gims-oss (github.com/BMA-Corgea/gims-oss, AGPL-3.0 — the same licence autoSQL ships under, so vendoring it is clean and citable). Verified on origin/main, not just on disk.

The skin risk inverts, and this is what the two panels below are for. Chunky pixel art with a hard black outline is at home on Classic and JRPG — those became the easy skins. The risk moved to the sober default: a smiling gnome beside a bubble on System/base, on a screen whose subject is “is this number wrong”. Two things answer it. He is not in every step — he appears at 1, 6 and 7 and is absent from the working steps 2–5. And step 6, where he does appear, is the reconciled moment: both panes read 123, the tour's path never fires the disagreement banner, so the one place he stands over a number is the place the number is right.

96px + a Win9x inset frame. JRPG gets the same rule with a gold frame.

A bitmap does not derive the way a token does — priced now rather than at build. Tokens recolour per skin; a PNG cannot. So what derives is the placement rule, not the artwork: five skins share one conservative treatment (62px, beside the bubble, no frame) because they share a sober register, and Classic and JRPG carry explicit rules for size, framing and presence — not colour. An eighth skin inherits the conservative treatment, which is the safe direction to fail in. What it does not get for free is a look tuned to its own personality; that is one rule per skin that wants one, and it is honest to say so rather than imply the token layer covers it.

The one thing that is not free

The demo has two ids and three data- attributes across all six .jsx files — there is nothing to anchor a spotlight to. The recommended fix adds data-tour="…" to about eight controls, which means editing files covered by manifest.json's ui:frontend-sources:sha256 and re-running ./run-demo build-ui to re-baseline the digest. That is the exact step T-14 skipped and T-16 had to repair, so it is on this page rather than in a commit. The overlay itself needs no .jsx: it builds its own DOM and appends to <body>, exactly as skin.js already does.
