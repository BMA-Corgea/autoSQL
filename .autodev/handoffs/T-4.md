# T-4 handoff — the timing run: framed, and waiting on a quiet machine and a number from Evan

**Where it is:** stage `sp-frame`, **framed**, `depends_on: ["T-1", "T-3"]`. The next stage is
`sp-investigate` — the benchmark itself. **Branch:** `main`. T-4 is the second of the two runs Evan
funded when he ruled NO-GO on T-1, and Q6 put it **after** T-3. **It waits for T-3 to *report*, not
to pass** — if T-3 fails, there is nothing to time and the answer is "do not run".

> **Vocabulary.** A **spike** is research that answers a question rather than shipping a feature
> (`sp-frame → sp-investigate → sp-synth → sp-decide → sp-spawn`); **`sp-frame`**, just finished,
> fixes the bar before any evidence exists. A **seat** is the agent working one stage of one ticket —
> the seat that wrote the framing is not the seat that runs the benchmark, and neither is Evan. An
> **arm** is one of the several ways the same dashboard widget is answered, timed side by side; the
> shippable one is **arm C**. A **corpus** is the pile of fake rows everything is measured over. A
> **negative control** is a deliberately broken input, used to prove the instrument can report a
> failure at all. **Recall** is the term the bar table turns on: *of the rows that should have been
> in the answer, how many actually came back.*

---

## What the run is

> **At realistic collection sizes, how many milliseconds does a person wait for one dashboard widget
> — on today's in-memory Python path, and on the compiled-SQL path — and is the compiled path's wait
> short enough to be worth building?**

Not "is it faster than Python". Not "by what multiple". **How many milliseconds.** That unit is
Evan's own, verbatim, under **GA-3**:

> *"Benchmark absolute user-facing latency rather than treating a 3.79×–7.15× relative slowdown as
> intrinsically fatal."*

His own numbers make the case better than any argument: at 20,000 rows the compiled path costs
**839 ms extra** (300.10 → 1,138.61 ms, 3.79×); at 1,000,000 rows it costs **51 seconds extra**
(8,331.43 → 59,590.03 ms, 7.15×). **The ratio moves by less than 2× across those rows; the wait moves
about sixty-fold.** A bar written in ratios says almost nothing about whether a person would mind.
Restated as the rule the write-up must obey: **a ratio may appear in the report; a ratio may never
appear in the verdict.**

**Full spec:** `spikes/T-1/EXPERIMENTS.md` §2 (it calls the run **E2**). **The bar, the hygiene, the
stop rules and everything below:** `spikes/T-4/FRAMING.md` (931 lines).

## The speed bars are a PROPOSAL. Evan has not accepted them.

This is the first thing to know about T-4's bar, and the framing says so itself. He set the
**direction** — milliseconds, not ratios — and never set the **number**. The three bars below were
ruled on his behalf while he was away, from measurements, and are wrap-up item 8 on his form:

| collection size | **PASS if median ≤** | **and the tail statistic ≤** | reps | today's Python, measured | is today's answer correct? |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| **20,000** | **350 ms** | 95th percentile 700 ms | n = 25 | 300.10 ms | **yes** — top-50 recall 100% |
| **100,000** | **1,000 ms** | 95th percentile 2,000 ms | n = 25 | 899.26 ms | **no** — recall 38% |
| **1,000,000** | **5,500 ms** | **worst of 9 — max ≤ 8,331 ms** | n = 9 | 8,331.43 ms | **no** — recall 4%; 48 of the 50 rows on screen do not belong, nor does the top one |

Measured as the **median** wall clock of one complete widget resolve on **arm C**, on a quiet host.
**Three bars rather than one, because what a person will wait for depends on how big a question they
asked** — a 20,000-row widget is a page load; a million-row widget is a report someone asked for
knowing it was big. **The 1,000,000-row bar is the binding one.** `EXPERIMENTS.md` §2.2 offers Evan
three candidate lines at that size — **2,500 ms** (proposed), **5,500 ms** (the earlier panel's floor
converted into his unit), or **8,331 ms** (a kill line rather than a bar) — and he picks one or names
his own.

**The hard kill condition, which is not a proposal:** at 100,000 and 1,000,000 rows the compiled
path's median must be **strictly below the Python path's median measured in the same session**.
Failing to beat what already exists is a kill whatever the absolute number. At 20,000 rows the test
is different, because today's answer there is already exactly right and the compiled path buys no
correctness: **no perceptible regression, within +100 ms of the same-session Python median.** Use the
same-session figure, never the recorded one — if it lands far from 8,331.43 ms, that is itself
evidence the host was not quiet, and the cell is void.

**One correction already applied to the bar, so nobody re-opens it:** the table originally demanded a
95th percentile at every size while ruling 9 repetitions at 1M — and **the 95th percentile of 9
samples is the maximum**, under every interpolation rule in common use. So the binding size's tail
bar would have been decided by one reading wearing the name of a percentile. Buying a real p95 at 1M
costs 100–156 minutes against a 2–3 hour total window — i.e. the rest of the run. The bar gave way
instead: at 1M it now reads **"worst of 9 — max ≤ 8,331 ms"**. Same number, same meaning, honest
label. Note it is **stricter** than the p95 it replaced, and that is only safe because a cell taken
outside the load band is **void** and re-run.

**Authority, checked line by line.** The unit is Evan's own under **GA-3** (recorded against T-1 — he
wrote the sentence, nothing is delegated). The authority for a seat ruling T-4's open questions while
he is away is **GA-5**, which names `["T-2","T-3","T-4"]`. **GA-4 is NOT the authority here** — it is
recorded against `["T-2"]` only and carries `scope_confirmed: false`; earlier drafts cited it and
that citation does not hold. None of the numbers move because of this; only the citation under them
changes.

## It needs the machine to itself — 2 to 3 hours, exclusive

`EXPERIMENTS.md` §2.6 budgets an **exclusive 2–3 hour window** for one measurement pass: three
required collection sizes, five arms, two widgets, one quiet host, including re-runs of voided cells.

**This is not a nicety.** A previous sweep on a busy machine came out **+246% to +282% wrong and
looked perfectly plausible**. The host is 20 cores / 46 GB, but it is **shared even though the
database no longer is**: `glp-strong-db` runs continuously, other project containers come and go, and
every one of them shares the same cores, RAM and single filesystem. A busy neighbour container
surfaces in `top` as an anonymous `postgres` process nobody connects to another project — **so check
the containers, not only the processes**, and read `glp_strong`'s commit count before and after each
size so that "GIMS was idle during the window" is a measurement rather than an assumption.

**Building T-2's demo is exactly the kind of work that voids these numbers.** The two cannot happen on
the same afternoon on this machine, and nobody has decided the order — wrap-up item 28. Evan naming a
window is wrap-up item 29.

**Disk is tight:** 19 GB free of 457 GB, **96% used**. The run needs ~1.2 GiB at peak. It fits, but
generate and drop CSVs one size at a time, and stop and report if free space falls below ~5 GB.

## The corpus must be rebuilt first — from `spikes/T-1/proto/REGENERATE-CORPUS.md`

**The file is `REGENERATE-CORPUS.md`.** Earlier drafts of the framing named a prerequisite called
`spikes/T-1/proto/CORPUS-REGEN.md`; **no file of that name has ever existed in this repo**. A run
opening by checking for its own stated prerequisite would have failed on a path that never existed,
and the natural reading of that failure — *"the notes were never written"* — would have been false at
the moment it was read. `spikes/T-1/proto/README-db.md` remains the anchor that points at wherever
these notes live.

The notes are 814 lines / 50 KB, written 2026-08-21 to discharge Evan's Q31 note (*"leave notes for
how to generate a corpus"*), and all six required content points check out: recreate the database and
install `runtime.sql` (§3–§4, verifying the `xpr` function count is **21**); the generator invocation
per size with **seed 1729 preserved** (§4); the two new fields `queue_depth` and `retest_count`
(**§0 states outright that the scripts do not produce them, and §7 gives the four-line patch** — an
honest documented gap, with the measured selectivity of 5.31% at 100,000 rows, inside the required
4.5–6.0% band); the loader invocation including the GIN index flag (§4); the `/dev/shm` fix for the
1M `VACUUM ANALYZE`, replaced by **`--shm-size=1g` at container creation** and verified end to end
(§8); and post-load verification — row counts, `content_md5`, `xor_sum`, sizes, and **303.2 bytes per
row** for the extended corpus (§5, §7).

**Three things the notes hand this run that change how its numbers must be read:**

1. **A cold rebuild reproduces the original corpus byte for byte** — heap+index at every size matches
   `analysis/measurement.md` §3 exactly, **700 MB (419 MB heap + 281 MB GIN)** at 1M included.
2. **But the corpus this run needs is NOT row-identical to the one the old numbers came from.**
   Adding the two fields shifts the random stream: measured over 1,000 rows, **only row 0 keeps its
   old values — 999 of 1,000 rows change.** Same shape, same generator, same distributions, different
   rows. **So no old absolute number may be quoted as though it had been measured on these exact
   rows**, including the date-widget control, whose entire value is that it is re-measured in-session
   under a recorded host load.
3. **The rebuild is cheap and its cost is measured, not guessed:** all six sizes in **under two
   minutes**, ~824 MiB of database, peak ~1.2 GiB with one CSV on disk at a time.

**Where it is rebuilt matters as much as that it is rebuilt.** The old scratch database `autosql_spike`
lived on `glp-strong-db`, the same container as Evan's live `glp_strong`, competing for the same
128 MB of buffer cache. It was **dropped at ~20:55 on 2026-08-21** with zero active connections, to
execute Q31 and to get this project's benchmark work off his live container for good — and the `xpr`
runtime schema went with it. **It is gone because it was deliberately dropped, not because a
container was rebuilt or lost.** Any document in this repo explaining its absence by a container
rebuild is wrong about the reason; any document still saying it exists is wrong about the fact.
`glp_strong` (95 MB) was never touched at any point. This run brings up its **own throwaway
container** — `autosql-corpus`, `pgvector/pgvector:pg16` (PostgreSQL 16.14, image cached locally),
`--shm-size=1g`, bound to `127.0.0.1:55434` — and destroys it with its volume afterwards. The server
settings recorded in the framing's environment table were **measured on `glp-strong-db`, i.e. the
wrong server**; re-read every one from `autosql-corpus` before timing anything.

## The negative control — required, and required BEFORE any millisecond is quoted

**Everything this run reports is a number of milliseconds. The one thing standing between a
dirty-host reading and a quoted headline is the *void path*** — the code that reads `/proc/loadavg`,
compares it to the load band, checks the row count, inspects the plan, and marks the cell **void**
rather than **measured**. **If that path never fires, the run emits a complete, plausible, internally
consistent table of timings with no indication whatsoever that anything was wrong.** That is a
description of the original sweep.

**This project has already shipped exactly this failure, and it stood for four passes.** A full
normal run of `proto/conformance.py` under a line tracer showed three of its four failure branches
with **hits=0** — dead surface — and nobody caught it until Evan asked for the check himself (Q4).

So, binding: before any real millisecond is quoted, the void path is **driven deliberately, through
the real harness** — not a copy of it, not a unit test of a helper.
`spikes/T-1/proto/conformance_injection_test.py` is a working template. One injection per
admissibility gate, each declaring in advance which outcome it must produce:

| # | injection | must produce |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | a `/proc/loadavg` reading above the ceiling — **injected; do not load the machine** | **void**, reason `host_load` |
| 2 | a table whose row count is deliberately short of N | **void**, reason `corpus_incomplete` |
| 3 | a plan containing an index-scan node other than the primary-key lookup | **void**, reason `index_help` |
| 4 | one arm's result perturbed by a single row | **void**, reason `arms_disagree` |
| 5 | a size never attempted at all | the **third** outcome, `not-attempted` — distinguishable from both of the others |

**Each injection must also be shown excluded from the statistics.** A cell that prints `void` and
still contributes to the median is *worse* than one that never voided, because it looks handled.
**If any injection comes back scored as admissible, the run reports nothing** — no caveat, no
footnote, no output; the control failure is what gets handed up instead. Cost: seconds on the
1,000-row table, against a 2–3 hour exclusive window.

## The risky part, for the next seat

> **The whole run turns on the host being quiet, and quiet is a state nobody can verify after the
> fact.**

Every other defect here is recoverable: a bar can be redrawn, a widget substituted, a statistic
recomputed, a ratio deleted from a sentence. **A cell measured on a busy machine is simply gone —
and it does not look gone. It looks like a number.**

So build the harness so that a cell measured on a dirty host is **visibly distinct** from a cell that
passed: three outcomes (**measured-and-admissible, measured-and-void, not-attempted**), never two.
Write the load reading into the same JSON object as the milliseconds, so the two cannot be separated
by anyone quoting one of them later. **A harness that records a timing without its host state
reproduces, inside this run, exactly the failure this run exists to correct.**

**And then prove the void outcome can actually be produced — that is the negative control above, and
it is not the same request.** The paragraph above is a *display* requirement, and a display
requirement cannot fail: a harness with beautifully distinct `void` styling and a void path that
never fires looks, from the outside, exactly like a harness that works.

## What else Evan may still change

- **The widget is INVENTED and must be labelled invented in every table, chart and sentence that
  reports a number for it.** His Q8 answer was *"I will name a real one I actually use"*; that was
  superseded by the second form's item 7, **"TAKE THE DEFAULT"**. Substituting a real one is a
  drop-in — nothing else about the run changes — with one mechanical constraint: it must stay inside
  the restricted subset, checked by machine, because measuring something nobody proposes to build is
  exactly what the last sweep did. Wrap-up item 30.
- **The rulings and their reverse costs** are `spikes/T-4/FRAMING.md` §10: R1 (25 reps at 20k/100k,
  9 at 1M; no column called "p95" below n = 20), R1a (the 1M tail bar restated as worst-of-9 — a
  genuine p95 there costs him a **second** exclusive window), R2 (measure buffer hits per cell; never
  write "warm" unmeasured), R2a (an untested size produces a **third** verdict, `INCOMPLETE` — never
  "pass with a gap"; `FAIL` outranks it), R3 (the prerequisite filename — resolved by inspection, not
  ruled), R5 (the negative control), and R4 (decision authority assumed `recommend-and-wait`, because
  the ticket carries no such field and nothing of Evan's speaks to it).
- **Extrapolating a missing cell is forbidden.** If the 1M load fails twice on shared memory, stop at
  100,000 rows and report the 1M row as **untested** — never as an estimate. That is exactly the
  defect flagged in the existing ≈16.7 s figure, which is arithmetic wearing a measurement's clothes.
